FANUC model:
The complete fanuc_crx10ia_support folder was copied byte-for-byte from
ENG_654's lectures_main/assets/models/fanuc_crx10ia_support, including its
crx10ial.urdf and all eight STL files. Seven main-chain visual meshes are used
at runtime; base_link_short.stl is retained as supplied. Mesh bounds were
inspected and are compatible with metres. URDF visual origins and scale are
applied separately from joint transforms. The URDF was not modified.
package:// mesh references resolve to the copied filename in this folder.
The viewer uses base_link and link_6/flange, not the fixed base/tool0 branches.
The supplied folder did not include a separate model license or attribution
file; its source and original package names are recorded here.

Asset exploration:
The supplied ENG_654 assets contain 68 STL meshes and 7 Collada DAE meshes,
including FANUC, ABB, and other robot models. No .blend, .obj, or .glb source
assets were found there. FANUC STLs are used for the actual robot; unrelated
ABB DAEs were not copied into a FANUC example.
assets/cup.glb is an original z-up teaching asset made from Three.js
LatheGeometry, TorusGeometry, and SphereGeometry and exported with the
library's GLTFExporter. Its orange dot makes orientation observable.
assets/door_panel.obj is an original 0.9 m x 1.8 m x 0.045 m teaching asset,
with its ideal hinge along local z. The interactive handle is at radius
0.85 m and height 0.9 m. Both assets can be imported into Blender for editing.
The three explanatory SVGs are original vector diagrams for this lecture.

Behavior:
All animation starts paused and requires Play or Animate assembly. Offscreen
scenes do not render. FANUC column assembly can continue while its matrix is
visible below the canvas; it pauses when the entire lab is offscreen or the
browser tab is hidden. Its timer is cleared on pause and scene disposal.
Scenes initialize as they approach the viewport and resize through
ResizeObserver. OrbitControls handles orbiting, zooming, panning, touch,
and keyboard panning. No custom camera/gesture controls are implemented.
Resource cleanup runs on page exit; normal browser back/forward caching is
preserved. Worked values and fallback descriptions accompany every lab.

Conventions:
V = [omega; v], W = [f; m], X = [xi; eta].
Frame superscript is the expression frame; right subscript identifies the body.
The power pairing is f dot v + m dot omega, not the ordinary six-vector dot
product. Force-first wrenches and angular-first twists transform with the
same numerical adjoint under this mixed pairing.
3J4 is 6 x 4. Determinant comparisons keep body 6 as the target.
Numerical matrices in screw_theory_math.js are row-major; conversion to
Three.js occurs through Matrix4.set. Angles enter the math in radians.

FANUC Jacobian laboratory:
Current joint origins and directions come from the actual URDF pose chain.
Each revolute screw is built as [e; p cross e] in the selected expression
frame and checked against the transported home screw. Empty column slots
are unrevealed values, not zero columns. Axis colors identify matrix columns.
Switching expression frames leaves the pose and physical point velocity
unchanged; a side-by-side comparison splits the adjoint into rotation and
origin correction. The pose controls retain the original PoE exploration.

Reading the sessions:
All eleven labs include static color/shape keys and native, expandable
symbol-and-unit glossaries in the lecture Markdown. These remain readable
if WebGL or a model cannot load. On initialization the key moves below the
primary controls (below the assembly buttons for FANUC). Frame-axis colors
are explained separately from physical vectors and the six FANUC joint
colors. Arrow scales, fixed motion rates, ghost objects, overlapping markers,
rounded readouts, and empty matrix slots are explained alongside the scene.
